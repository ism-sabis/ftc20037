## Our Robot {#robot}

Our front intake pivots up and down with gravity helping it move. Three rows of surgical tubing guide game elements through the robot. A pair of spring-mounted flywheels can spread apart to passively accommodate both POLLEN and NECTAR, so the same launcher can handle either type. Added mass is intended to help the flywheels retain speed between shots; the motors will maintain a target RPM, and a rear flipper controls when an element is released.

Our turret uses a Limelight to help aim the launcher, and an adjustable hood changes the shot trajectory. For a HIVE score, the turret shoots directly toward the HIVE. For a FLOWER score, a slide-out guide redirects the shot through the top of the FLOWER. A separate servo-positioned ramp inside the FLOWER lets POLLEN slide out through the bottom.

Our chassis uses mecanum wheels and pocketed 7075 aluminum panels, and the robot weighs about 27 lb. For autonomous movement, we plan to use an odometry pod and IMU with Pedro Pathing.

## Outreach {#outreach}

This season, we visited [Tolomatic's facility in Hamel, Minnesota](https://www.tolomatic.com/contact-us/) for a design review with an applications engineer, a controls engineer, and a mechanical engineer. They gave us feedback on our robot and showed us around the facility. It was a great opportunity to learn from engineers in industry.

We're also in communication with [99 Technologies](https://www.99technologies.com/), which is helping us find ways to make our robot code more efficient. More outreach updates will follow as the season continues.
